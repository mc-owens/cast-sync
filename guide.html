Help & Guide

A task-oriented reference for the full director workflow. For quick help on any page, click the ? button in the top bar.

Getting Started
How CastSync works

CastSync is organized around a production. Everything -- auditions, casting, rehearsals, attendance -- lives inside one production at a time. You can have multiple productions across one or more organizations.

The typical workflow moves through four phases:

  • Collect auditions -- build your form, share the join code, and let dancers submit their availability
  • Build your cast -- review availability, schedule rehearsal blocks, and assign dancers to pieces
  • Plan rehearsals -- fill out the production timeline with recurring blocks, special events, and room assignments
  • Communicate -- publish the cast list so dancers can see their schedule, send emails, and manage day-to-day notes and attendance

These phases overlap in practice. You can start scheduling before auditions close, or send a partial cast list while still casting other pieces.

Setting up a new production
  • Create the production from the Organizations page
  • Set the production name, start date, end date, and audition date in Production Settings
  • Build your audition form in Audition Form
  • Share the Join Code from Production Settings so dancers can find the form
Collect Auditions
Building the audition form

The Audition Form builder lets you add, remove, and reorder fields. Standard fields (name, email) are always included. Common additions include piece preferences, experience notes, headshot upload, and availability.

The availability field is the most important one: it generates the color-coded grid in Casting Availability. Dancers mark which days and times they are free and that data drives the entire availability analysis.

Sharing the form with dancers

Find the Join Code in Production Settings. Share it with dancers -- they enter it on the CastSync auditionee portal to find and complete your production's form. Each production has its own unique code.

You can control whether the form is open or closed from the Submission Window section of Production Settings. Set a deadline to close automatically, or toggle manually at any time.

Reviewing submissions

All submissions appear in Auditionees. From there you can see each dancer's availability data, notes, and audition number (if your form includes one).

Audition numbers are useful for numbered audition formats where directors track dancers by number rather than name during the audition itself.

Build Your Cast
The two casting tools and when to use each

Cast Builder is for creating a new rehearsal block and casting it in one step. Pick a piece, pick a time window, see who is available, select your dancers, and click "Add to Schedule." The block and the casting are both saved at once.

Casting Availability is for reviewing availability across the whole cast before making decisions. The colored grid shows every dancer's availability for each rehearsal slot at a glance. You can add dancers to pieces from here too.

In practice: use Casting Availability to study the picture, then use Cast Builder (or Cast List) to finalize assignments.

Cast members vs understudies

Cast members are the primary performers. Understudies learn the role and cover if needed. Both are stored per piece and both appear on the published cast list.

You can drag dancers between Cast Members and Understudies in Cast List at any time. This does not affect rehearsal block assignments.

Adjusting casting after the fact

Go to Cast List to add or remove dancers from a piece, assign named roles, reorder the display, and manage understudies. You can also add more dancers from Casting Availability at any point.

Plan Rehearsals
The Production Timeline

The Production Timeline is your week-by-week rehearsal grid. Columns are days of the week; rows are rooms. Drag on any empty cell to create a rehearsal block, resize from the right edge to adjust duration.

Click any block to open the detail drawer. From there you can: assign the block to a piece, change the room, view cast members for that piece, log attendance, check for approved absences, and save notes.

Rooms

If your production uses named rooms, set them up in Production Settings. Once rooms are configured, each block in the drawer shows a room assignment and the grid rows represent individual rooms.

The org overlay shows other productions' blocks as subtle background elements. This helps you spot double-booked rooms across your organization before it becomes a problem.

Special Events

Add one-time events in Production Settings: performances, tech rehearsals, dress rehearsals, spacing rehearsals, fittings, company meetings, and more. Each event has a date, optional time, optional location, and a scope (full cast, specific pieces, or staff only).

Events marked "visible to dancers" appear in dancer schedules. Performances show with a gold highlight on the timeline; other events appear as colored markers at the top.

Run Your Production
Attendance

Log attendance from the Attendance page or from the drawer on the Production Timeline. Select a rehearsal date and piece, then mark each dancer present, absent, or late.

Dancers with approved absence requests are flagged automatically so you can quickly distinguish excused absences from unexcused ones.

Absence Requests

Dancers submit absence requests from their portal. Review them in Absence Requests. Approving a request marks it as excused in Attendance and shows a callout in the Production Timeline drawer for that rehearsal date.

Production Notes vs My Private Notes

Production Notes are shared with all directors and staff on this production. A notification email goes out when a note is posted (configurable in Production Settings). Use these for information the team needs to see.

My Private Notes are only visible to you. When saving a note from the Production Timeline drawer, you can choose which destination to use. Notes saved from the drawer are automatically prefixed with the piece name, day, and date for context.

Faculty and staff

Add staff members (choreographers, rehearsal directors, etc.) to individual pieces from Cast List -- expand a piece card and use the Production Staff section. Staff get a limited view of the app: only their assigned pieces, no production-wide settings or auditionee data.

Communicate with Dancers
Publishing the cast list

The Publish to Dancers toggle in Cast List controls whether cast results are visible in the dancer portal. While set to Draft, nothing is visible. Once Published, dancers can see which pieces they are in.

Publishing requires two things to be true before dancers see their schedules: casting must be published AND "Enable My Rehearsals" must be turned on in Production Settings under Student Access.

Emailing the cast

Click Email Cast in Cast List to send the cast list to all auditionees. Add an optional message at the top of the email for rehearsal expectations, required attire, or what happens next.

For a formatted, themed version of the cast list (with your choice of header color, suitable for printing or sharing as a PDF), use Design & Export.

What dancers see in their portal

Once casting is published and My Rehearsals is enabled, a cast member sees:

  • Their rehearsal blocks for pieces they are cast in, organized by week
  • Special events marked "full cast" or scoped to their pieces, if visible to dancers
  • A "Next Up" summary showing the next scheduled rehearsal

Dancers do not see other people's casting, production-wide schedules, or any director tools.
